# Issue Tree Patterns — Reference

Typical MECE decompositions by problem type. Use as starting points — adapt to the specific question.

---

## Market Entry: "Should Company X enter Market Y?"

```
Should Company X enter Market Y?
├── H1: The market is attractive enough to justify entry
│   ├── H1a: Market size is large enough (absolute minimum: EUR [X]B)
│   ├── H1b: Growth rate is sufficient (>5% CAGR)
│   └── H1c: Profitability is achievable (industry margins >10%)
├── H2: Company X can win in this market
│   ├── H2a: Company X has a differentiated value proposition
│   ├── H2b: Company X has the capabilities needed to compete
│   └── H2c: Competitive position is achievable within 3-5 years
└── H3: Entry economics make strategic sense
    ├── H3a: Entry cost (capex, time) is justified by NPV
    ├── H3b: Risk/return profile is acceptable
    └── H3c: Entry mode (organic/JV/acquisition) is available
```

**Critical path:** Usually H1 and H2 — these drive the go/no-go. H3 is refinement.

## Growth Strategy: "How should Company X grow?"

```
How should Company X grow?
├── H1: Grow within existing markets (penetration)
│   ├── H1a: Increase market share from existing customers
│   └── H1b: Capture new customers in existing segments
├── H2: Enter adjacent markets (extension)
│   ├── H2a: Expand to new geographies
│   ├── H2b: Expand to adjacent customer segments
│   └── H2c: Expand to adjacent products/services
└── H3: Enter new markets (diversification)
    ├── H3a: Acquire position in unrelated market
    └── H3b: Create new market through innovation
```

**Ansoff framing:** Use this when strategy is unclear. H1=penetration, H2=market/product development, H3=diversification.

## Cost Reduction: "Why is cost too high, and how to reduce?"

```
Why is cost too high?
├── H1: Cost structure is higher than competitors
│   ├── H1a: Labor cost per unit is above benchmark
│   ├── H1b: Material/procurement cost is above benchmark
│   └── H1c: Overhead allocation is disproportionate
├── H2: Processes are inefficient
│   ├── H2a: Duplication of work across functions/geographies
│   ├── H2b: Manual processes where automation is viable
│   └── H2c: Bottlenecks causing waste and rework
└── H3: Organization is misaligned with delivery model
    ├── H3a: Span of control creates management overhead
    └── H3b: Decentralization creates non-scalable fixed costs
```

## Acquisition: "Is Target Z a good acquisition for Company X?"

```
Is Target Z a good acquisition?
├── H1: Strategic rationale is compelling
│   ├── H1a: Acquisition accelerates strategy vs. organic
│   ├── H1b: Acquisition provides capabilities X cannot build
│   └── H1c: Market timing makes acquisition superior to waiting
├── H2: Target is attractive on its own merits
│   ├── H2a: Business quality is high (model, margins, moat)
│   ├── H2b: Management team is strong and retainable
│   └── H2c: No material hidden risks (commercial, legal, operational)
└── H3: Deal economics are attractive
    ├── H3a: Valuation is justified at expected synergies
    ├── H3b: Synergies are achievable and well-scoped
    └── H3c: Integration risk is manageable
```

## Revenue Decline: "Why is revenue declining, and what to do?"

```
Why is revenue declining?
├── H1: External market factors
│   ├── H1a: Market demand is shrinking
│   ├── H1b: Competitors are taking share
│   └── H1c: Regulatory change is reducing addressable market
├── H2: Internal commercial issues
│   ├── H2a: Product/service is losing relevance
│   ├── H2b: Sales force effectiveness has declined
│   └── H2c: Pricing is uncompetitive
└── H3: Customer dynamics have shifted
    ├── H3a: Customer concentration — key accounts leaving
    └── H3b: Cohort retention declining (churn problem)
```

## Adaptation Notes

These patterns are **starting points only.** Always adapt:
1. Remove branches that are clearly irrelevant to the specific question
2. Add branches specific to the industry or company context
3. Adjust depth based on available data — don't build 4 levels if you can only fill 2
4. Label the critical path explicitly before delegating research
