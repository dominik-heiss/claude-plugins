# Cognitive Biases in Consulting Analysis — Reference

The most common reasoning failures in consulting work, and how to counter them.

---

## Confirmation Bias
**What it is:** Seeking evidence that confirms the hypothesis; discounting or ignoring contradictory evidence.

**How it shows up:** Sara finds 3 sources supporting H1, ignores the one that contradicts it. Tom's issue tree is built to prove the pre-agreed conclusion.

**Counter:**
- Explicitly assign someone to find evidence AGAINST each major hypothesis
- "Devil's advocate" role in every QA review
- In source reviews, specifically ask: "What does the evidence say against our working assumption?"

---

## Anchoring
**What it is:** Over-weighting the first piece of information encountered; subsequent analysis adjusts insufficiently from that anchor.

**How it shows up:** The first market size estimate (EUR 40B) anchors all subsequent work, even when better data emerges showing EUR 25B.

**Counter:**
- Explicitly revisit the first estimate after Phase 1 research is complete
- Force a "hypothesis update" checkpoint — treat initial hypotheses as provisional
- Ask: "If we were starting today with what we know now, what would our hypothesis be?"

---

## Availability Bias
**What it is:** Overweighting information that is easy to recall or retrieve; underweighting what requires effort to find.

**How it shows up:** Using the most recent industry report (easy to find) as the primary source; ignoring primary research that contradicts it.

**Counter:**
- Source hierarchy: primary > secondary > tertiary. Don't let convenience substitute for rigor.
- Ask: "Is this source easy to find because it's authoritative, or because it's promoted?"

---

## Sunk Cost Fallacy
**What it is:** Continuing down an analytical path because significant effort has already been invested, even when evidence suggests the direction is wrong.

**How it shows up:** Week 3 of research; data contradicts H1 but team continues because "we've spent so much time on it."

**Counter:**
- Explicit hypothesis update checkpoints — force a stop-and-reassess
- Frame sunk cost explicitly: "We've invested X hours on H1. The evidence contradicts it. The right call is to update the hypothesis now, not after more investment."

---

## Narrative Fallacy
**What it is:** Creating a coherent story from data points that are not actually causally connected.

**How it shows up:** "Revenue declined in Q3, and we launched a new product in Q2 — the product launch caused the decline." (correlation, not causation)

**Counter:**
- Always distinguish correlation from causation in the analysis memo
- Ask: "What other explanations for this pattern exist?"
- Test the causal claim: "What would we expect to see in the data if [cause] → [effect] is true?"

---

## Planning Fallacy
**What it is:** Underestimating time, cost, and risks of proposed actions; overestimating benefits.

**How it shows up:** Business case projects 3-year payback; historical comparables show 5-7 years for similar initiatives.

**Counter:**
- Always include a "reference class" comparison: what did similar initiatives achieve?
- Financial Modeler: document every assumption; conservative as base case
- QA Reviewer: explicitly check whether projections are inside or outside historical ranges

---

## In-Group Bias / Groupthink
**What it is:** Team converges on a shared view without sufficient challenge; dissenting views are suppressed.

**How it shows up:** Working session concludes quickly because everyone agrees. Real concerns never surface.

**Counter:**
- Partner Advisor and QA Reviewer roles exist specifically to counter this
- Engagement Manager should explicitly ask: "What's the strongest argument against our current recommendation?"
- Create psychological safety for disagreement in working sessions
