# Source Hierarchy — Reference

How to evaluate, weight, and cite sources in consulting research.

---

## Tier 1: Primary Sources (Highest reliability)
- **Company filings** (10-K, annual reports, earnings calls, investor presentations)
- **Government statistics** (Eurostat, OECD, national statistics offices, regulatory databases)
- **Original primary research** (surveys, interviews, proprietary data collection)
- **Academic peer-reviewed research** (if recent and methodology is sound)

**Use for:** Core facts, market sizing anchors, financial claims
**Cite as:** "[Source name] ([year]), [specific figure]"

---

## Tier 2: Credible Secondary Sources
- **Tier-1 consulting firm reports** — McKinsey Global Institute, BCG Henderson Institute, Bain & Company research, Deloitte Insights, PwC Strategy& — rigorous methodology but check for bias
- **Tier-1 research firms** — Gartner, Forrester, IDC, IBIS World — use for market sizing; check if estimates are based on primary research or synthetic models
- **International organizations** — IMF, World Bank, IFC, ECB — strong on macro; sometimes lagged
- **Quality financial press** — Financial Times, Wall Street Journal, Bloomberg, The Economist — use for current events and trend signals, not for precise quantification

**Use for:** Market context, trend signals, competitive intelligence
**Cite as:** "[Source name] ([year]), [report title]"

---

## Tier 3: Directional Sources (Use with caution)
- **Industry associations** — useful for trend direction; often show optimistic picture; methodology frequently opaque
- **Trade press** — useful for current events and expert quotes; reliability varies by publication
- **Company-sponsored research** — usually directionally correct; check who funded and why
- **Analyst reports** — sellside has optimistic bias; note the analyst's position

**Use for:** Trend confirmation, leads for primary sources
**Never use for:** Core financial claims, market sizing without triangulation

---

## Tier 4: Leads Only (Not citeable evidence)
- Wikipedia, general web searches
- Blog posts, opinion pieces, LinkedIn articles
- Undated or unknown-author content
- AI-generated content

**Use for:** Finding leads to credible sources
**Never cite directly in deliverables**

---

## Bias Assessment Matrix

| Source Type | Common Bias | Weight Adjustment |
|------------|-------------|------------------|
| Industry association | Upward market size, positive growth outlook | Treat as upper bound |
| Consulting firm report | Complexity bias, engagement-seeking framing | Check methodology; often reliable for sizing |
| Government statistics | Lagged (6-24 months), conservative | Reliable for structure; flag vintage |
| Sell-side analyst | Optimistic on covered companies | Useful for industry color; discount quantitative forecasts |
| Trade press | News value bias (extreme events get coverage) | Good for signals, not for base rates |

---

## Citation Standards in Deliverables

**In research briefs:** `(SRC001)` after each claim
**In presentations:** Footnote with source ID and title
**In financial models:** Source cell reference in assumption sheet
**Source-registry:** Always complete — this is the audit trail

Never reference "industry sources" or "market research" without a specific source ID. If you can't cite it, you can't use it.
